The Demographic Divide

While global life expectancy has surged over the last two decades, a universal biological and social gap persists: women consistently outlive men.


Across our dataset, this survival advantage averages nearly 4.8 years. This phenomenon cuts across all income brackets, driven by a complex mix of genetic resilience, occupational hazards, and behavioral risks. Wealth shifts the baseline, but the gender gap remains stubbornly intact.

Special Case · High GDP, Low Life Expectancy

The Wealth Paradox: When GDP Fails to Buy Time

A high national income generally guarantees a longer lifespan—until it doesn't.

Consider our primary anomaly for 2021: The Bahamas. With a GDP per capita exceeding $30,000, it ranks among the wealthiest nations in the Americas. Yet, its life expectancy stalls at just 70.4 years, significantly lagging behind its economic peers.

The culprit is the "Survival Filter" of Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) combined with stark economic inequality. The Bahamas' GDP is heavily inflated by luxury tourism and offshore banking, masking a high Gini coefficient.

Wealth does not automatically translate to accessible preventive healthcare. When extreme income disparity meets an environment saturated with imported processed foods, the result is a staggering 20.4% mortality rate from chronic lifestyle diseases before age 70.
